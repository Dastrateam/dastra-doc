# Introduction to Privacy

**Privacy**, or personal data protection, refers to the rules, principles and practices that guarantee respect for private life and give individuals control over their personal data.\
It is now at the heart of digital transformation, the development of artificial intelligence and responsible data governance.

***

## The European framework: from the GDPR to the AI Act

The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**, applicable since **25 May 2018**, is the cornerstone of data protection in the European Union.\
It governs the processing of personal data of people located in the European Union. It also applies to organizations established outside the EU when they offer goods or services to these people or monitor their behavior.

> [Official GDPR text on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32016R0679)

The GDPR introduced:

* a logic of **proactive accountability**;
* the obligation to **document compliance**;
* **stronger rights** for individuals (access, rectification, erasure, portability);
* **dissuasive sanctions**, up to €20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

Its purpose is twofold:

1. **Return control** of personal data to European citizens.
2. **Harmonize** protection rules to facilitate economic exchanges in the single market.

***

## An integrated approach to data governance

Since 2018, the European framework has grown into a **set of rules on data**:

| Text                             | Main objective                                                      | Date of application                             |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **GDPR**                         | Personal data protection                                            | 2018                                            |
| **Data Governance Act (DGA)**    | Facilitate secure sharing of public and private data                | 2023                                            |
| **Data Act**                     | Guarantee portability and fair access to industrial data            | 2025                                            |
| **AI Act**                       | Regulate the development, deployment and use of AI systems          | From 2025, in stages                            |
| **NIS 2 / Cyber Resilience Act** | Strengthen the security of information systems and digital products | NIS 2: 2024; Cyber Resilience Act: 2026 to 2027 |

***

## The AI Act: governing artificial intelligence

The **EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act)**, adopted in 2024, establishes the first global legal framework for AI. It entered into force in August 2024 and its obligations apply in stages.\
It requires you to **document, assess and monitor** AI systems according to their risk level. The exact obligations depend on the role of the organization (provider or deployer) and on the risk level of the system.

To prepare, organizations should in practice:

* **inventory their AI systems** in an internal register;
* **qualify their risk level** (minimal, limited, high, prohibited);
* **document the purpose, the training data and the controls**;
* **carry out regular audits**;
* **inform users** and guarantee transparency.

Dastra includes an AI systems register that supports these needs. See [AI systems](../features/ai-systems/README.md).

## A global movement

Data protection now extends beyond the European Union.\
Many countries have adopted laws close to the GDPR:

| Region         | Key text                                                                                                                      |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Canada         | _Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act_ (PIPEDA) and Quebec's Law 25                                   |
| United States  | _California Consumer Privacy Act_ (CCPA), amended by the _California Privacy Rights Act_ (CPRA), _Colorado Privacy Act_, etc. |
| United Kingdom | _UK GDPR_ and _Data Protection Act 2018_                                                                                      |
| Brazil         | _Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados_ (LGPD)                                                                                       |
| Japan          | _Act on the Protection of Personal Information_ (APPI)                                                                        |
| Singapore      | _Personal Data Protection Act_ (PDPA)                                                                                         |

These laws share common principles: transparency, security, minimization and accountability.

***

## Obligations of organizations

In practice, the GDPR and related texts require organizations to:

* **appoint a data protection officer (DPO)** in the cases set out in Article 37;
* **keep a record of processing activities** (Article 30);
* **implement technical and organizational measures** to guarantee security (Article 32);
* **carry out a data protection impact assessment (DPIA)** for processing likely to result in a high risk (Article 35);
* **notify personal data breaches** to the supervisory authority and, when the risk is high, to the data subjects (Articles 33 and 34);
* **document AI systems** and their risks (AI Act);
* **raise awareness and train** teams.

A proactive approach to compliance makes it easier to demonstrate that compliance to customers, partners and authorities.
